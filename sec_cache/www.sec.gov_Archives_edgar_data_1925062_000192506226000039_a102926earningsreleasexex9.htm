FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE — CHEVY CHASE, MD, October 29, 2026
Fellow Shareholders,
Forbright, Inc.(1) (Nasdaq FRBT), the parent company of wholly-owned subsidiary Forbright Bank (the "Bank"), today reported financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.
Last quarter, we committed to writing to you plainly, so we will start with the headline: this was a strong quarter. The Company met or exceeded nearly every key performance metric we track relative to our expectations for the quarter. In addition, we believe the business is well-positioned for a strong finish to 2026 and a strong start to 2027.
Looking ahead, we believe our credit discipline, commitment to process and expense management, efficient digital funding platform, and focused lending strategies and fee businesses position the Company very well for long-term growth.
We will begin, as we should, with the numbers for this quarter:
•Net income attributable to the Company was $20.6 million, or $0.41 of diluted earnings attributable to the Company per outstanding share for this quarter.
•Loans grew 9.5% — from $6.1 billion last quarter to $6.6 billion this quarter.
•Net Interest Margin of 3.18% was roughly flat this quarter, compared to 3.19% last quarter.
•Credit quality was excellent. Loans on non-accrual were down, our average loan risk rating improved, and our core net charge-off rate was down to 3 basis points from 8 basis points in the prior quarter.
•Future growth is supported by $7.6 billion in deposits and $1.9 billion in cash and investments at quarter-end, excluding $744 million of deposits and $721 million of cash classified as held-for-sale.
While current conditions remain favorable, we endeavor to operate the business prudently and believe we are well-positioned to adapt to changing economic conditions.

BALANCE SHEET SUMMARY
Total assets increased $1.1 billion to $9.6 billion as of September 30, 2026, from $8.5 billion as of June 30, 2026. The transaction will include branches in Potomac and North Bethesda, Maryland, a customer service hub in McLean, Virginia, and local deposits with a September 30, 2026 value of $744 million. We expect the Branch Sale to receive regulatory approval during the fourth quarter of 2026 and close during the first quarter of 2027. The increase in assets was primarily due to a build-up of cash, primarily recognized in assets held-for-sale, in anticipation of the Branch Sale and loan growth, partially offset by a decrease in investment securities.

OUR VIEW
This quarter demonstrates the strength of our business model and continued execution against our growth strategy.
Lending: Loan growth was 9.5% QoQ, driven by over $1 billion in originations. Growth was particularly strong in our Healthcare Finance strategy and each of our six lending businesses enters the fourth quarter with a strong pipeline. Spreads remain very competitive, and we continue to focus on conservative deal structures.
Fee Businesses: Our FHA/HUD strategy had a good quarter, including some catch-up from a slower second quarter. As noted last quarter, we expect results in this business to vary meaningfully QoQ. Our other fee businesses performed generally in line with plan. We have strategic and operational work to do to re-start growth in Alliance Partners, and that work began this past quarter. Higher long-term rates are expected to extend the duration of the servicing book in Solar Servicing.
One note on presentation in our fee businesses: Solar Servicing amounts are increased both in non-interest income and non-interest expense by reimbursable expenses of $7.2 million this quarter, with no impact on Forbright's earnings. As master servicer, we pass certain expenses (including legal expenses) through to the owners of the loans we service. That has always been the case, but extensive litigation in the residential solar sector has magnified the effect in a meaningful way.
Deposits and liquidity: As previewed in last quarter’s earnings presentation, the deposit promotion capability we launched in June exceeded expectations. We ended the quarter with $1.9 billion in cash and investments (excluding $721 million of cash held-for-sale associated with the pending Branch Sale transaction) which is 19.8% of assets. This liquidity position allowed us to hold our deposit rates steady following the Fed's September rate hike, which benefits net interest margin as approximately 85% of our loans repriced between the September rate hike and mid-October. We also have ample liquidity to fund the Branch Sale. Our digital deposit platform is designed to give us flexibility and control over our funding by raising deposits in times of strong loan growth and the ability to manage our cost of funds with discipline when we have excess liquidity.
Credit: Credit quality was excellent, and our average loan risk rating improved again this quarter. Forbright’s national, sector-focused commercial lending capabilities provide attractive financing solutions for our clients. At the same time, we manage credit risk by using our expertise and rigorous diligence to make critical and objective assessments of the risk in each loan we make.
Expenses and Efficiency: Our expense management efforts continue to pay off, and we are on track to meet or exceed our 2027 expense and efficiency objectives. Our efficiency ratio improved to 69.65% this quarter compared to the prior quarter. That number includes $7.2 million of Solar Servicing reimbursable expenses reflected as non-interest expense and non-interest income on our income statement. As we look to 2027, we expect operating expenses — excluding reimbursable expenses in our Solar Services business — will largely remain flat despite the projection for strong loan growth. This is due to the benefits of our fully built platform, a strong company-wide expense management culture, and efficiencies from AI adoption across the business.

AI AND THE FUTURE OF DEPOSITS
As previewed last quarter, we believe AI will accelerate the shift of deposits from branches to digital platforms. A good example of this is the recent launch of a personal AI assistant from a global social media company that quickly became one of the fastest-growing AI apps ever launched. It is an early indicator of a world where AI agents will allow consumers to shop for good savings rates; move money to attractive yield; and strip away the inertia that has long kept cheap and sticky consumer deposits in brick and mortar branches.
For banks, we believe an advantage will shift to institutions with attractive rates, the lowest-friction onboarding technology and an agent-ready platform. Forbright, Inc. Chairman and CEO, John Delaney recently co-wrote in the Wall Street Journal: this is "unambiguously good [for consumers]" but it also raises the bar for banks, and it is the shift Forbright is intently focused on.
Our Digital Banking team plans to roll out new AI capabilities in 2027 that are designed to allow Forbright customers to experience the benefits of AI without compromising financial security. For example, we plan to introduce a virtual card that customers can assign to their AI agents. This new feature is designed to help our customers keep their bank accounts private while securely making purchases through their AI agents. Our upcoming Virtual Card for AI agents is expected to allow users to set strict dollar limits and receive real-time updates, so people can have their AI agent order food delivery or make retail purchases without having to offer their banking history or debit cards. We also plan to give our customers the ability to provide their AI agents with permissioned, logged, and read-only access to Forbright data.
We view Forbright as well positioned for the AI-related changes coming to banking, and we intend to make sure it stays that way.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures
(Unaudited)

This earnings release contains “non-GAAP financial measures” within the meaning of Item 10(e) of Regulation S-K. Non-GAAP financial measures are financial measures that are not presented in accordance with GAAP. We use these non-GAAP financial measures in the internal evaluation of our performance and management of our business as well as to explain our results of operations to stockholders and the wider investment community. The following non-GAAP financial measures appear in this earnings release:
•Tangible common equity - We calculate tangible common equity by deducting goodwill and other intangible assets from stockholders' equity.
•Tangible common equity per common share - We calculate tangible common equity per common share by dividing tangible common equity, as defined above, by our total common shares outstanding for the period, excluding the dilutive effect of outstanding stock options, and including the effect of outstanding shares from restricted stock awards.
•Return on average tangible common equity - We calculate return on average tangible common equity by dividing adjusted net income for the period by average tangible common equity over the same period. Adjusted net income, used for the calculation of return on average tangible common equity, is calculated by adding back the tax effected amount of intangible asset amortization to net income.
•Non-core trust administration - We calculate non-core trust administration by deducting fixed monthly trust administration fees from trust administration, a component of other non-interest income as reported in the footnotes to the Consolidated Financial Statements. Non-core trust administration represents the reimbursements we receive for various expenses incurred in administration of the trusts.
•Non-core gains/(losses) on sales of loans and investment securities, net - We calculate non-core gains/(losses) on sales of loans and investment securities, net by deducting gains from sales of loans to Alliance Partners from gains/(losses) on sales of loans and investment securities, net, as reported on the Consolidated Statements of Income.
•Core and non-core non-interest income - Core non-interest income equals total non-interest income less (a) non-core trust administration, (b) non-core gains/(losses) on sales of loan and investment securities, net, (c) unrealized gains on loans and financing receivables, net, (d) rental income and (e) other income. Non-core non-interest income equals total non-interest income less core non-interest income.
•Adjusted total revenue - We calculate adjusted total revenue by deducting non-core non-interest income from total revenue.
•Pre provision net revenue - We calculate pre provision net revenue by adding provision for credit losses to income before income taxes.
Our management believes that these non-GAAP financial measures and the information they provide are useful to investors because these measures allow investors to view our performance in the same manner our management evaluates performance. Although we believe these non-GAAP financial measures are useful in evaluating our performance, these non-GAAP financial measures should not be considered in isolation or as a substitution for the most directly comparable or other financial measures presented in this earnings release under GAAP. Additionally, the manner in which we calculate these non-GAAP financial measures may be different from how other companies calculate financial measures with similar names.
