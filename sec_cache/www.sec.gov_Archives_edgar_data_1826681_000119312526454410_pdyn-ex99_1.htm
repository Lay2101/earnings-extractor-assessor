Palladyne AI Reports Record Third Quarter 2026 Results

Revenue increased 895% year-over-year and 48% sequentially to $8.6 million

Raises bottom end of full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $25 million while reiterating top end of $27 million (377% - 415% growth year over year)

Expiration of warrants and contingent shares results in reduction of fully diluted shares by 9%

SALT LAKE CITY – November 10, 2026 – Palladyne AI Corp. (NASDAQ: PDYN) (“Palladyne” or “the Company”), a U.S.-based technology company delivering intelligent autonomy software and mission-critical components for the defense and advanced manufacturing sectors, today announced financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.

Ben Wolff, President and Chief Executive Officer of Palladyne AI, commented:

“We generated record revenues in the third quarter of $8.6 million, an increase of 895% year over year and up 48% sequentially. Compared to Q2, we saw a 26% increase in revenues associated with our components business, driven in part by our first delivery of our next generation BRAIN FC-1 flight computers. Our revenue from government contracts increased 135% quarter over quarter. Given our current trajectory, we are raising the low end of our full-year revenue guidance to $25 million, while reiterating the top end of $27 million.

"Backlog at the end of the quarter was $18.7 million, of which $15.3 million is funded, reflecting signed net new contracts for $2.7 million in the quarter. Despite the natural timing fluctuations in backlog as customers utilize remaining funding on existing contracts before executing renewals, and continued uncertainty surrounding defense budgets, our backlog remained materially higher than at the start of both Q1 and Q2.

"Operationally, our technology is proving itself in the field. At the Army's Project Convergence Capstone 6, SwarmOS again integrated with Anduril's Lattice and enabled multi-drone, multi-vendor autonomous operations in front of the U.S. Joint Force and allied partners. Most importantly, SwarmOS was evaluated on nine key performance criteria by the U.S. Army, and we received the highest rating possible on all nine. SwarmOS’ ability to deliver intelligent collaborative autonomy works as advertised and is exceeding our expectations.

“We shipped more BRAIN flight computers during the quarter than ever before, including the first units of our BRAIN FC-1 variant, which is our cost optimized mission grade flight control computer, demonstrating our ability to commercialize and scale production efficiently and cost effectively.

Exhibit 99.1

“We entered into agreements to extend SwarmOS to fixed-wing aircraft with Orkid Tech and to NORDA Dynamics' combat-proven Underdog module. After quarter end, we launched the joint development of SwarmShot, a counter-UAS swarm system, with Federal Ammunition and Mossberg, and BRAIN was named a finalist in the Army's FUZE xTech Apex Intercept competition. Our IAI partnership continues to gain visibility and attract interest from key constituents. On the commercial side, our collaboration and joint sales motions with FANUC America, a global industrial automation leader with over 40 million products installed worldwide, is putting our software in front of a host of manufacturing companies ranging from Fortune 500 companies to small and mid-sized businesses.

"We are investing to capture this demand. Operating cash burn1 decreased sequentially, driven by our strong revenue performance, but remained elevated due to continued research and development across all our product lines and working capital investments to fund revenue growth. We expect full-year burn to land close to or slightly above the high end of our guided range, largely due to opportunistic capex purchases, with fourth-quarter burn below our year-to-date quarterly average due to revenue and margin improvements. With $47.0 million in cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities, we believe we are well funded to execute, and we expect our 2026 growth to extend into 2027."

1The Company defines operating cash burn as cash used in operations plus capital expenditures (capex).

Third Quarter 2026 Financial Highlights (vs. third quarter 2025)

•
Revenue increased 895% to $8.6 million compared to $0.9 million;
•
Operating loss of ($13.4) million compared to ($8.1) million, mainly reflecting higher stock-based compensation, added headcount to support growth, and higher research and development costs related to Gremlin-X development;
•
GAAP net loss and basic and diluted GAAP loss per share (EPS) of ($12.0) million and ($0.25), respectively, compared to ($3.7) million and ($0.09), respectively, in the prior year period
•
Third quarter 2026 included a $1.1 million non-cash gain on warrant liability revaluation, compared to a gain of $3.8 million in the prior year period;
•
Third quarter 2026 included $4.8 million in non-cash stock-based compensation expenses, compared to $1.1 million in the prior year period;
•

•
Cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities totaled $47.0 million as of September 30, 2026, up from $43.7 million as of June 30, 2026;
•
Backlog as of September 30, 2026 decreased 24% during the quarter to $18.7 million, reflecting approximately $8.6 million of revenue recognized, partially offset by approximately $2.7 million in net new contract awards; and
•
All 20.5 million warrants, representing the right to purchase approximately 3.4 million shares, and all 2.3 million remaining contingent merger consideration shares issued or issuable in connection with the Company’s de-SPAC transaction, expired unexercised or unissued on September 24, 2026.

The Company now expects full-year 2026 operating cash burn to be close to or slightly above the high end of its ($32.0) – ($36.0) million range. Operating cash burn for the first nine months of 2026 included ($1.8) million of capital expenditures.

NON-GAAP FINANCIAL MEASURES

(Unaudited)

 

To supplement our financial statements presented in accordance with GAAP and to provide investors with additional information regarding our financial results, we have presented in this release non-GAAP net loss and non-GAAP net loss per share (non-GAAP EPS), each of which are non-GAAP financial measures. Non-GAAP net loss and non-GAAP EPS are not based on any standardized methodology prescribed by GAAP and are not necessarily comparable to similarly titled measures presented by other companies.

We define non-GAAP net loss as our GAAP measures of net (loss) income excluding the impacts of stock-based compensation expense, gain on change in fair value of warrant liabilities, expenses related to business combinations and other non-recurring or non-operating expenses. We define non-GAAP EPS as non-GAAP net loss divided by weighted average outstanding shares.

The most directly comparable GAAP measures to non-GAAP net loss and non-GAAP EPS are net (loss) income and EPS, respectively. We believe excluding the impact of the previously listed items in calculating non-GAAP net loss and non-GAAP EPS can provide a useful measure for period-to-period comparisons of our core operating performance. We monitor, and have presented in this release, non-GAAP net loss and non-GAAP EPS because they are each a key measure used by our management and board of directors to understand and evaluate our operating performance and to establish budgets. We believe non-GAAP net loss and non-GAAP EPS help identify underlying trends in our business that could otherwise be masked by the effect of the expenses that we include in net (loss) income but not in non-GAAP net loss. Accordingly, we believe non-GAAP net loss and non-GAAP EPS provide useful information to investors, analysts and others in understanding and evaluating our operating results, enhancing the overall understanding of our past performance.

Non-GAAP net loss and non-GAAP EPS are not prepared in accordance with GAAP and should not be considered in isolation of, or as an alternative to, measures prepared in accordance with GAAP. There are a number of limitations related to the use of non-GAAP net loss and non-GAAP EPS rather than net (loss) income and EPS, which is for each the most directly comparable financial measure calculated and presented in accordance with GAAP. In addition, the expenses and other items that we exclude in our calculations of non-GAAP net loss and non-GAAP EPS may differ from the expenses and other items, if any, that other companies may exclude from non-GAAP net loss and non-GAAP EPS when they report their operating results, limiting the usefulness of non-GAAP net loss and non-GAAP EPS for comparative purposes.

In addition, other companies may use other measures to evaluate their performance, all of which could reduce the usefulness of non-GAAP net loss and non-GAAP EPS as tools for comparison.
